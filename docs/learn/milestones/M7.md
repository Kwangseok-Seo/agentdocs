# M7 — The tree

> Roadmap: M7

## What was done

The Entries pane is a tree. A Bundle opens onto its supporting files, and a Source that walks a whole directory — a project's `docs/` — opens onto its directories, level by level. Every row starts closed; `l` or Right opens the selected one, `h` or Left closes it, or goes up to its parent from a row with nothing showing below it, and Enter does either. ## Concepts picked up here

[[recursive-data]] · [[option-and-match]] (`None` against `Some` of nothing; the `if let` a new variant slips past) · [[borrowing]] (a borrow ends at its last use) · [[lifetimes]] (a borrow handed out through an argument; `Hit<'a>`, `Row<'a>`) · [[testing]] (a path held open)

Used early and only noted: `HashSet`, whose `insert` and `remove` each answer whether anything changed; `Vec::retain`; `find_map`, to stop at the first supporting file with a Hit; `Option::is_some_and`; and `OpenOptionsExt::share_mode` on Windows, for the tests.

## Pitfalls actually hit

Each is written up where the concept lives:

- [[recursive-data]]: a level taken for everything below it — one wrong answer printed `docs:17` for cli-maker's 80, and two more followed from the same picture, which was M6's code: `absorb` had poured everything below into one list.
- [[option-and-match]]: `None` read as *there is nothing inside*, four times across slices 2 and 3; and, **not settled**, `if let … else` expected to stop at E0004 when a variant is added.
- [[borrowing]]: a borrow taken to last as long as its variable; `.clone()` on a `&Path` expected to end it.
- [[ownership]]: `insert` takes the path it is given, so the toggle's logic was right and the build was not (E0382).
- [[lifetimes]]: **not settled** — a visible `&` taken as a safe return, four times in one direction.

One more belongs to this program rather than to Rust. Asked how `retain` should take a Bundle's Lead out of its supporting files, the pick was `entry.name != "SKILL"`. It removed nothing: the Lead's frontmatter had already renamed its Entry — to `alpha` in the test — and the build also warned `unused variable: lead`. A name is a label the file gives itself; where the file is, is its path. Two review questions went wrong the same way before a drawing of the label and the arrow, side by side, set them apart.

Two things were new in how the quizzes went. When the same wrong answer came back a third and a fourth time, more explanation of the same kind did nothing; a diagnostic question did — *what do you take `None` to mean*, *what is dropped when a function ends* — and found the layer: an everyday reading of a keyword, and a criterion not yet held. And the assistant's own words could steer an answer: slice 3's *"while `rows` is in use"*, and a question in slice 4 whose option said, truly, that a `Vec` is dropped — what was dropped was only the box of arrows.

In cli-maker the 16 ADRs used to be followed by 64 pages of a learning wiki in one list; in ai-bitcoin-paper two rows read `README` with nothing to tell them apart, and in the tree they sit under `adr/` and `db/`.

**What a tree shows.** Markdown files, and the directories that hold one somewhere below — the rule every Walk already used for files. Opened all the way, `session-retro` went from 17 rows on disk to 7 — and to 5 once hidden directories were left out, below — and `synced` from 256 to 25; 117 of the rows left out were `.xsd` files. `synced` has no Lead at its top, and eight skills two levels down, each with its own `SKILL.md`: listed as one row with a `-` until now, they can be read in the tree. Shown two captures, one with it and one without, the author chose to leave such directories out, as a `.txt` is left out, and not to count them.

## Decisions

[ADR-0009](../../adr/0009-unreadable-is-a-row-where-it-was-found.md): **what a Walk could not read is a row where it was found**, and a document that will not open is one of those rather than an Entry. It replaces the third paragraph of [ADR-0006](../../adr/0006-unreadable-is-counted-not-dropped.md), which listed what *unreadable* covers and left the third case out — written at the author's choice, over recording the change in this log alone.

- **Chosen by the author**: `docs/` as a tree, not only Bundles; directories named with a leading dot left out; a supporting file's Hit marked with its row's name; the ADR.
- **Chosen by the assistant and reported**: a tree holds Markdown files and the directories that hold one; a link standing in for a Bundle is not opened; every row starts closed, `l`/Right open, `h`/Left close or go up, Enter either, in the Entries pane only; which rows are open is a `HashSet<PathBuf>` in the App — by path, so a row stays open while another Source is looked at, and apart from what the Walk found, which the screen only reads; the Lead taken out by its path, at the top level only; a Bundle kept when each word is in its Lead or any supporting file, and its Hit from the Lead first; an unreadable row for a document, a Bundle whose Lead will not open, and inside a Bundle whose directory will not open; the count derived from those rows.

The existing records were checked against the new surface. [ADR-0001](../../adr/0001-read-only-viewer.md): opening a row only reads. [ADR-0005](../../adr/0005-sources-report-their-own-directory.md): a supporting file belongs to its Bundle's Source, and nothing is merged. [ADR-0007](../../adr/0007-links-are-listed-not-followed.md) covers the new walk inside a Bundle — `supporting` walks with `md_tree`, which enters no link — and a linked Bundle's `inside` is `None`, never asked. Its sentence naming `md_files`, `md_tree` and `bundle_dirs` as where `file_type()` is asked still holds: since slice 4b they ask through `described`, and nothing else calls `described`. [ADR-0008](../../adr/0008-screen-for-a-bare-command-at-a-terminal.md) is untouched. In `CONTEXT.md`, **Hit** and **Unreadable** were redefined in slice 4.

## What review caught

Through slice 4 there was no separate reviewer. Mutation runs, the corpus and fixtures found, in the assistant's own work: the two mutation counts above; slice 0's theory placing the junction that made 128 rows in M3, when M1 found it and M3 ended it; and questions whose wording leaned toward a wrong answer, above.

Closing the milestone meant listing what M7's new facts made untrue, and that found five more. [ADR-0006](../../adr/0006-unreadable-is-counted-not-dropped.md) defined *unreadable* by two cases and there are three — hence ADR-0009. [[testing]] said nothing automated could build a directory that refuses to be read; `hold` has since slice 4b. [[lifetimes]]' table of every function returning a borrow had `first_hit`'s old signature and none of the thirteen M7 added. [[iterators]]' table had `first_hit` where `own_hit` now is. And the README's search sample said `skills:0/6`, where it is now `1/6`. A pass over every earlier log's *Left for later* found one item out of the chain: M2's, on showing that two rows are the same file, which no log after M2 carried.

Then two reviewers worked in copies of the working tree, one on the code and one on these documents, and the assistant reproduced what they reported before acting on it. The code review found:

- **`h` inside a Bundle that would not open closed the Bundle.** The row saying its directory would not open carries the Bundle's own path, and `close_row` asked only whether that path was open — so `h` on the row closed its parent and left the selection on whatever row slid into its place, the next Bundle, whose preview then showed. `close_row` now asks whether the row opens at all, as `toggle_row` already did — slice 5, with a test that fails on the code before it.
- **Six mutations passed all 251 tests**: `h` going to the row above rather than the parent, since every tree test was one row wide; `l`, and Enter, opening a row with nothing below; a supporting file's mark taken from its file name, since no test file had been renamed by frontmatter; a leading dot looked for anywhere in a path; and files with a leading dot left out. Slice 5 adds tests for them. One is caught no more: once `h` asks whether a row opens, a row with nothing below put in the set of open rows by `l` changes nothing on screen.
- Three smaller things, none of them anywhere in the corpus, are in *Left for later*: a Lead spelled `skill.md`, a Bundle whose Lead will not open printed like one whose directory will not open, and a directory named with a dot directly in a Bundle Source.

The document review found two numbers in this log that something had changed after they were measured: `session-retro` opening to 7 rows, counted before hidden directories were left out — it opens to 5 — and this repository's `docs:42`, counted before this log and the two pages written with it existed. Both are corrected above. It reproduced the rest: the test counts at every commit, the three listing hashes, the listing samples line for line, every function in [[lifetimes]]' table and no others, the two compiler messages it re-ran, and the chain of *Left for later* across all seven earlier logs.
